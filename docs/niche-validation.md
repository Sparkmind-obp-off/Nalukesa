# Niche Discovery & Validation Playbook

## Purpose
Compare niche opportunities without confusing broad market popularity with a realistic opportunity for this specific account, creator, product, and platform.

## Initial lanes
### 1. Beauty & personal care
Possible angles: routine education, ingredient/feature explainers, product texture or use demonstration, honest FAQs, practical tools.
Check: product authenticity, seller reliability, claim substantiation, sensitive-skin context, returns, current affiliate terms, and platform restrictions.
Never promise medical outcomes or repeat unsupported skincare claims.

### 2. Everyday fashion
Possible angles: styling combinations, fit context, fabric/detail demonstrations, versatile essentials, honest value comparisons.
Check: sizing, color accuracy, return conditions, stock stability, image/video permissions, and seller quality.

### 3. Useful home goods
Possible angles: everyday friction, use-case demonstration, storage/organization, feature comparison, practical limitations.
Check: product function, durability evidence, safety where relevant, shipping, seller quality, and whether the benefit is clear on video.

Fragrance is a possible later lane, but do not prioritize it merely because it sounds attractive. Check audience fit, authenticity, platform eligibility, margins, and differentiation first.

## Research sequence
1. **Define audience and problem.** Who has the problem? In what situation? What do they currently use?
2. **Collect primary evidence.** Record exact listing URLs, dates, visible price, review count/rating when available, seller identity, stock/offer details, and affiliate terms.
3. **Collect demand signals.** Look at repeated questions, recurring complaints, purchase-intent language, content engagement patterns, and product-level marketplace signals. Save source links and dates.
4. **Collect competition signals.** Identify recurring formats, common claims, creator saturation, content quality gaps, and differentiation opportunities.
5. **Check economics.** Estimate commission only from current verified terms. Consider price changes, cancellations, returns, content effort, and approval conditions.
6. **Check feasibility.** Account eligibility, ability to obtain product or demonstrate it honestly, permitted content/assets, production time, and platform rules.
7. **Score confidence, not just opportunity.** A high score with weak sources should remain low-confidence.
8. **Create a bounded experiment.** One audience/problem, a small set of products, a small set of creative angles, a fixed window, and a pre-written decision rule.

## Evidence quality labels
- **A — Primary verified:** current official affiliate terms, actual dashboard data, official listing, or directly observed analytics, with timestamp.
- **B — Direct observation:** visible listing, public comments, review patterns, or content examples with links and timestamps; useful but not proof of sales.
- **C — Secondary report:** credible industry report with publication date and methodology.
- **D — Anecdotal / unclear:** unverified claims, screenshots without context, vague trend claims, or unsupported estimates.

Never promote a D-level claim into a verified metric. Multiple weak signals do not automatically become strong evidence.

## Niche scorecard (0–5 per dimension)
- Audience pain / clarity
- Product discovery breadth
- Offer economics
- Content demonstration potential
- Competitive differentiation
- Seller and product trust
- Platform and compliance feasibility
- Production sustainability

For every score, store evidence, source URL, date checked, confidence, and a short explanation. Use “unknown” when evidence is absent; do not silently score unknown as average.

## Suggested first validation sprint
- Days 1–3: gather evidence for all three lanes, including at least 5 relevant product listings per lane if practical.
- Days 4–5: compare offer terms, audience questions, content saturation, trust, and ability to produce content without spending.
- Days 6–15: choose a manageable test scope. Publish only on eligible accounts and in line with platform rules. Keep creative experiments distinct enough to learn.
- Days 16–23: review retention/engagement if available, qualified clicks, orders, approved commission, cancellations, and production effort.
- Days 24–30: decide scale / iterate / hold / stop. Document what would change the decision.

The number of listings and days are working guidelines, not statistical guarantees. If data is sparse, extend the observation window or report inconclusive.

## Replace a lane when
- Product supply or affiliate eligibility is not workable.
- Trust or compliance risk is too high.
- No meaningful audience problem or content angle can be identified.
- The test is repeatedly infeasible at the available budget/time.
- Another lane has materially stronger evidence and can be tested fairly.

## Research record minimum
Every claim must have: claim, source, URL or locator, date accessed, evidence grade, observed fact, interpretation, and caveat.
